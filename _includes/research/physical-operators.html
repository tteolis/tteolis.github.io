Learning physical operators

How can a learned model predict the evolution of a physical system across different initial states and resolutions? I study neural operators for kinetic, fluid, and wave dynamics, with the goal of building reusable, structure-preserving models for physical simulation.

Stochastic jump transformer

A standard transformer moves each token to a new state. A physical collision, however, can have several possible outcomes even for the same incoming pair. In work with Maarten de Hoop, we introduce the stochastic jump transformer: attention updates the context, a learned interaction rate determines whether a pair interacts, and a learned jump map determines the outcome. Randomness is part of the interaction law, rather than an approximation to deterministic attention.

This gives a kinetic route to physics foundation models: learn local interaction laws and compose them across particles and time. The architecture can encode physical structure directly; for example, elastic pair maps preserve momentum and energy exactly. Our theory characterizes the continuous conditional laws represented by the architecture and connects small-step compositions to nonlinear drift–jump dynamics. Spatially mollified Maxwell–Boltzmann dynamics provide a concrete physical example.

T. Teolis and M. V. de Hoop. In-context Maps as a Collision Operator: Stochastic Jump Transformer. Preprint.

Markov Kernel Networks

Neural operators should respond stably when the same physical input is sampled on a different grid or point cloud. With David Mis, I. Dokmanić, Matti Lassas, and Maarten de Hoop, we develop Markov Kernel Networks as a common framework for transformers, Fourier neural operators, Flowers, and their hybrids. The networks alternate spatial information mixing through Markov kernels with pointwise channel mixing.

Our representation theorem characterizes uniformly discretization-stable operators as stable Markov Kernel Networks. A variational description of the read kernels then gives a way to recover familiar architectures and design new combinations. We are developing and training hybrid models at scale on acoustic wave datasets, with applications to wave propagation, Helmholtz equations, and inverse problems.

S. D. Mis, T. Teolis, I. Dokmanić, M. Lassas, and M. V. de Hoop. All Roads Lead to Markov Kernel Networks: A Representation Theorem for Discretization-Stable Learning. Preprint. Related work: Hybrid operator learning of wave scattering maps in high-contrast media.
